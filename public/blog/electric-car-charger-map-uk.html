UK electric car charging points map: find public chargers

Search public EV charging points on a live UK map, then use this guide to filter by connector and speed, understand payment rules, and plan motorway rapid charging.

Finding a usable public charger is harder than spotting a pin on a map. Speeds, connectors, opening hours and whether the unit is actually working all vary. A charger map is the starting point: search a postcode or current location, filter what your car can use, then treat live status as a clue rather than a guarantee.

UK public charge point operators must publish certain data and meet consumer rules set out in the Public Charge Point Regulations 2023 (GOV.UK). The Department for Transport also publishes official statistics on public EV charging infrastructure. As of 1 July 2026, DfT counted 121,171 public EV chargers in the UK, of which 28,887 were rated rapid or above (50kW+). The government’s former National Chargepoint Registry was withdrawn in December 2024; operators are instead required to share open data such as location, real-time availability, connector types and payment methods.

How to find and filter public chargers

Use a map in this order. The same steps work on Autodun EV Finder and on other open-data maps.

  1. Open the live map at ev.autodun.com.
  2. Search a postcode, town or your current location.
  3. Filter by the connector your car actually uses (Type 2, CCS or CHAdeMO), not by brand.
  4. Filter by speed for the stop: standard overnight or shopping stays, or rapid/ultra-rapid for a short motorway break.
  5. Open the site card and check access hours, network, and how you pay.
  6. Treat “available” as a clue. On a longer trip, pick a backup charger within easy range.

Operators must make specified data public using the Open Charge Point Interface (OCPI), including location, connector types and payment methods (Public Charge Point Regulations 2023). Maps still lag when a bay is blocked, a cable is missing, or a feed is stale.

Connector types

Your car will only charge if the plug matches. The Competition and Markets Authority’s electric vehicle charging market study summarised the usual UK split:

  • Type 2 — the usual connector for slow and fast AC charging. Most modern EVs sold in the UK use Type 2 for AC.
  • CCS (Combo 2) — the usual connector for rapid and ultra-rapid DC charging on newer cars.
  • CHAdeMO — an older DC rapid connector still found on some networks and some earlier Nissan and Mitsubishi models.

UK alternative-fuels infrastructure rules require AC normal and high-power charging points to have Type 2 connectors, and DC high-power points to have a CCS Combo 2 connector (AFIR report 2023). Older CHAdeMO-only bays can still exist. Filter the map rather than assuming every rapid pin will fit.

Charging speeds

Speed is the charger’s maximum power, not a promise of what your car will draw. The Department for Transport’s EV charging statistics FAQ (updated 21 May 2026) uses these public-charger power bands:

DfT power band Rating Typical use (DfT)
Standard 3 kW to less than 8 kW Long stay — day or overnight; often on-street
Standard plus 8 kW to less than 50 kW Medium stay — shopping or leisure
Rapid 50 kW to less than 150 kW Short stay
Ultra-rapid 150 kW and above Short stay — the main purpose of the stop is to charge

Source: GOV.UK — Electric vehicle charging infrastructure statistics: frequently asked questions. DfT notes that the car, battery management, load sharing and grid supply can all deliver less than the advertised rating.

As of 1 July 2026, DfT reported that 49% of UK public EV chargers were in the standard band (3 kW to less than 8 kW), 27% standard plus, 12% rapid and 12% ultra-rapid (public EV charging infrastructure statistics: 1 July 2026). A dense map of slow on-street posts is not the same as a usable rapid stop.

Costs and payment

Home and public charging are priced differently. We have not published unverified “typical” pence-per-kWh figures here because they vary by network, membership and time of day.

Home charging uses your domestic electricity tariff. Off-peak rates, where you have them, usually make overnight charging cheaper than a public rapid session — but the exact pence-per-kWh figure is on your energy bill, not on a charger map.

At public charge points, the Public Charge Point Regulations 2023 require the total rate of a charging session to be displayed clearly in pence per kilowatt hour, including connection or transaction fees in that p/kWh figure. Rapid chargers usually cost more per kWh than slower units. Some sites remain free, but that is no longer the default. Confirm the price on the unit, in the operator’s app, or on-site signage before you start a session.

You do not always need an app. GOV.UK guidance says contactless payment must be available on new public charge points of 8 kW and above, and on existing public charge points of 50 kW and above, without a pre-existing contract with the operator (Public Charge Point Regulations 2023 guidance). Slower or older units may still need a network app or RFID card. Keep more than one payment option.

Motorway and rapid charging

For a motorway trip, filter the map for rapid or ultra-rapid DC (50 kW and above) and for CCS if that is what your car uses. DfT classes only 11% of public EV chargers as en-route as of 1 July 2026 — including motorway service areas and rapid or above chargers at hospitality sites (DfT statistics, 1 July 2026).

Rapid networks also have a 99% reliability duty under the 2023 regulations. That is a yearly average across an operator’s rapid network, not a promise about the bay in front of you. See EV charging reliability UK for what the duty covers, how blocked bays are treated, and how to report a fault.

Practical motorway habit: choose a primary stop and a backup within easy range, arrive with enough charge to divert, and do not rely on a single pin. Step-by-step planning is in how to find reliable EV chargers on UK motorways.

What is an electric car charger map in the UK?

An electric car charger map in the UK is a digital map that shows where public electric vehicle charging points are located. It lets drivers see nearby chargers, connector types, and — when the operator has updated its feed — whether a point is working. These maps are usually websites or apps.

Most maps also show charging speed, network operator, access rules, and whether payment is required. Live availability helps you avoid a bay that is already in use, especially in busy towns or at motorway services. It is still a snapshot, not a reservation.

Home electric car chargers vs public charging points

Home electric car chargers and public charging points meet different needs, and many UK drivers rely on a mix of both. A home charger is typically installed on a driveway or private parking space and allows the vehicle to charge overnight. This is often the most convenient option, as the car is ready to use each morning without needing to visit a public location.

Public charging points are for when you are away from home or do not have private parking — car parks, shopping areas, workplaces and motorway service stations. Speeds range from slower units for longer stays to rapid chargers for shorter stops. Pricing and payment methods differ by network and location.

For drivers without a home charger, public charging is essential for everyday use. Even drivers with home charging still rely on public chargers for longer journeys. Understanding the difference helps you choose based on parking access and how you drive.

Are there electric car charger grants available in the UK?

Yes, for some households. The Office for Zero Emission Vehicles (OZEV) publishes current schemes on GOV.UK — Electric vehicle chargepoint grants (updated 1 April 2026). Owner-occupiers of houses with a private driveway are generally not the target of the home grants below.

  • Renters and flat owners: up to £500 towards installing a chargepoint if you own and live in a flat, or rent any residential property, and you have private off-street parking.
  • Households with on-street parking only: up to £500 where you are installing a cross-pavement solution (for example a charging gully) and you only have on-street parking.

GOV.UK states these home and workplace grants have been extended for a final year, with schemes available until 31 March 2027 and new grant rates from 1 April 2026. Apply through an OZEV-authorised installer. Always read the live eligibility rules before you book work — grant schemes change.

Grant amounts, payment rules and charger availability can change. Confirm details on GOV.UK and with the charge point operator before you travel or book an installation. Electric cars still need an MOT — see what is checked on an EV MOT in 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free electric car charger map for the UK?

Yes. Autodun’s live UK map at ev.autodun.com is free to use. Official government statistics on public chargepoints are published by the Department for Transport. Operators must also make certain chargepoint data open under the Public Charge Point Regulations 2023.

How do I find and filter public charging points?

Open a charger map, search a postcode, town or your current location, then filter by the connector your car uses and the speed you need. Check access hours and payment method before you set off, and keep a backup site on longer trips because live status can lag.

What connector types do UK public chargers use?

Most UK AC public chargers use a Type 2 connector. Rapid and ultra-rapid DC chargers typically use CCS (Combo 2), with some older DC units still offering CHAdeMO. Your car must match the connector unless you use a compatible adapter. Filter the map by connector rather than assuming every pin will fit.

Do I need an app to use public electric car chargers?

Not always. GOV.UK says contactless payment must be provided for certain public charge points (new public charge points of 8kW and above, and existing charge points of 50kW and above), without a pre-existing contract. Slower or older units may still need a network app or RFID card. Having more than one payment option remains useful. Source: Public Charge Point Regulations 2023 guidance.

How much does public EV charging cost in the UK?

There is no single national tariff. The Public Charge Point Regulations 2023 require the total rate of a charging session to be displayed clearly in pence per kilowatt hour. Rapid chargers usually cost more per kWh than slower units. Confirm the price on the unit, in the operator’s app, or on-site signage before you start.

How do I find rapid chargers on UK motorways?

Filter a charger map for rapid or ultra-rapid DC (50kW and above) near motorway service areas, and always pick a backup stop. DfT statistics class only some public chargers as en-route. Live status is a clue, not a guarantee. See Autodun’s motorway charging guide for planning backups and queues.

Are electric car charger maps accurate?

Most charger maps are generally accurate, but information can sometimes be out of date. Availability and status depend on how often charging networks update their data. It is a good idea to check recent updates or have a backup option when planning longer journeys.

Can I rely on public chargers if I don’t have a home charger?

Many UK drivers rely entirely on public charging, especially in cities and flats. Public chargers can support day-to-day driving, but availability and cost vary by location. Planning ahead using a charger map is important to avoid delays.
