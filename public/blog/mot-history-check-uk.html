How to check a vehicle’s MOT history in the UK

By Autodun Research Team · Updated 9 November 2026

The official way to check a vehicle’s MOT history is the free GOV.UK MOT history service, run by the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA). You only need the registration number (number plate). People often search for a “DVLA MOT checker”; MOT records are a DVSA service on GOV.UK, not a DVLA checker.

The record is useful if you own the vehicle and want to know when the next test is due, or if you are buying used and want to see past failures, recorded mileage and recurring problems. Autodun can present the same DVSA data in a simpler timeline — it does not replace the official record.

How to check MOT history on GOV.UK

These steps follow the official Check the MOT history of a vehicle service.

  1. Open gov.uk/check-mot-history.
  2. Enter the vehicle’s registration number (number plate). GOV.UK says this is all you need.
  3. Review the past MOT results, including whether each test passed or failed.
  4. Check the mileage recorded at each test and when the next MOT is due.
  5. If the tests were done in England, Scotland or Wales, you can also download certificates and see which parts failed or had minor problems. To see where each test was done, you will need the 11-digit number from the vehicle’s log book (V5C).

Coverage depends on where and when the vehicle was tested (GOV.UK):

  • Cars, motorcycles and vans: results for tests done since 2005.
  • HGVs, trailers, buses and coaches: results for tests done since 2018.
  • Northern Ireland: results for tests done since 2017. You cannot download MOT certificates through this service; if the MOT is still valid, apply for a duplicate on NI Direct.

What the MOT history record shows

According to GOV.UK, you can check past MOT results, including:

  • whether the vehicle passed or failed
  • the mileage recorded when it was tested
  • when the next MOT is due

Your MOT history may also tell you if the vehicle has been recalled for a safety reason, depending on the manufacturer. GOV.UK also links to a separate recall check for a vehicle model, part or accessory.

For tests in England, Scotland or Wales, GOV.UK says you can also:

  • download a copy of the current and previous MOT certificates
  • see what parts failed at each test, and if any parts had minor problems
  • see where each test was done (you will need the 11-digit number from the V5C)

The MOT history record does not usually confirm:

  • whether recommended repairs were actually completed (ask for receipts or a service history)
  • accident, finance or ownership history (those are separate checks)
  • the vehicle’s condition between tests — faults can appear at any time

MOT history vs MOT status

MOT status answers a narrower question: does the vehicle currently have an MOT certificate, and when does it run out? Use the official GOV.UK MOT status service. You still only need the registration number.

MOT history is the timeline: past pass or fail results, recorded mileage and, where available, defects and advisories. Status tells you if it is currently legal to drive (subject to the other rules). History helps you judge how the vehicle has been maintained.

GOV.UK also says you cannot drive or park a vehicle on the road if the MOT has run out, except to go to a pre-arranged MOT test or to or from somewhere to be repaired. You can be fined up to £1,000 for driving without a valid MOT (Getting an MOT).

How to read advisories and failures

Advisories are not automatic failures, but they matter. A single advisory may be normal wear. Repeated advisories for the same area can mean the issue was never properly fixed.

For the DVSA’s three-tier defect system (Dangerous, Major, Minor) and how advisories sit alongside it, read MOT advisories explained.

  • Look for repeats: the same area (for example tyres, brakes, suspension, lights or emissions) year after year.
  • Check escalation: an advisory that later becomes a failure.
  • Compare mileage: consistent year-to-year progression is what you want to see.

If the vehicle failed and you need to know whether a retest is free, see MOT retest costs and DVSA retest rules and the official MOT testing guide (accounts and fees).

Red flags to watch for when buying used

Be cautious if you see:

  • Multiple failures for the same component area (for example brakes or emissions)
  • Large mileage jumps or long gaps with no records
  • Repeated advisories with no improvement year on year
  • A sudden “clean” result after years of repeats, with no evidence of work done

How to spot possible mileage clocking from the MOT record

  • Year-on-year decreases: a later MOT showing lower mileage than the year before is a major warning sign.
  • Implausible jumps: very small mileage over many years followed by a big jump, or the reverse.
  • Gaps with low later readings: long periods with no MOTs then an implausibly low mileage figure.
  • Cross-check: compare the DVSA record with service invoices and previous adverts. If you cannot reconcile the numbers, walk away.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check MOT history on GOV.UK?

Go to gov.uk/check-mot-history and enter the vehicle’s registration number (number plate). The official DVSA service is free. You will see past pass or fail results, the mileage recorded at each test, and when the next MOT is due.

What does an MOT history record show?

GOV.UK says the record includes whether the vehicle passed or failed, the mileage recorded when it was tested, and when the next MOT is due. For tests in England, Scotland or Wales you can also download MOT certificates, see failed and minor items, and (with the 11-digit number from the V5C) see where each test was done. A recall may also be shown, depending on the manufacturer.

Is MOT history the same as MOT status?

No. MOT status tells you whether a vehicle currently has an MOT certificate and when it runs out. MOT history shows past test results, recorded mileage and, where available, defects and advisories over time.

Can I check MOT expiry date online?

Yes. GOV.UK MOT history includes when the next MOT is due. You can also use the MOT status service if you only need to know whether the vehicle currently has a certificate and when it runs out.

Is it illegal to drive without an MOT in the UK?

In most cases, yes. GOV.UK says you cannot drive or park the vehicle on the road if the MOT has run out, and you can be fined up to £1,000. The exceptions are driving to a pre-arranged MOT test, or to or from somewhere to be repaired (Getting an MOT).

How do I spot possible mileage issues on the MOT record?

Compare the mileage recorded at each MOT. A later test showing a lower mileage than an earlier one is a serious warning sign. Also look for implausible jumps or long gaps followed by a surprisingly low reading. Cross-check the DVSA record with service invoices and previous adverts. If you cannot reconcile the numbers, do not buy.
