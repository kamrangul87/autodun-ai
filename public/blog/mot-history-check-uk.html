How to check a vehicle’s MOT history in the UK (free on GOV.UK)

By Autodun Research Team · Last updated: 23 November 2026

The official way to check a vehicle’s MOT history is free: the GOV.UK MOT history service, run by the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA). You only need the registration number (number plate). People often search for a “DVLA MOT checker”; MOT records are a DVSA service on GOV.UK, not a DVLA checker.

Use it if you own the vehicle and want the next due date, or if you are buying used and want past failures, recorded mileage and recurring problems. Autodun can present the same DVSA data in a simpler timeline — it does not replace the official record.

How to check MOT history on GOV.UK

These steps follow the official Check the MOT history of a vehicle service.

  1. Open gov.uk/check-mot-history.
  2. Enter the vehicle’s registration number (number plate). GOV.UK says this is all you need.
  3. Review past MOT results, including whether each test passed or failed.
  4. Check the mileage recorded at each test and when the next MOT is due.
  5. If the tests were done in England, Scotland or Wales, you can also download certificates and see which parts failed or had minor problems. To see where each test was done, you will need the 11-digit number from the vehicle’s log book (V5C).

Coverage depends on where and when the vehicle was tested (GOV.UK):

  • Cars, motorcycles and vans: results for tests done since 2005.
  • HGVs, trailers, buses and coaches: results for tests done since 2018.
  • Northern Ireland: results for tests done since 2017. You cannot download MOT certificates through this service; if the MOT is still valid, apply for a duplicate on NI Direct.

What each part of the record means

According to GOV.UK, you can check past MOT results, including:

  • Pass or fail — the result of that test, not a running condition report between tests.
  • Mileage — the odometer reading the tester entered at that MOT.
  • Next MOT due — when the current certificate runs out, if the vehicle has one.

Your MOT history may also tell you if the vehicle has been recalled for a safety reason, depending on the manufacturer. GOV.UK also links to a separate recall check for a vehicle model, part or accessory.

For tests in England, Scotland or Wales, GOV.UK says you can also:

  • download a copy of the current and previous MOT certificates
  • see what parts failed at each test, and if any parts had minor problems
  • see where each test was done (you will need the 11-digit number from the V5C)

DVSA’s MOT testing guide states that the electronic MOT record is the legal record of the test. A paper certificate is a view of that record. The record relates only to testable items at the time of the test. It is not evidence of condition at any other time, of general mechanical condition, or that the vehicle meets every construction-and-use rule.

The MOT history record does not usually confirm:

  • whether recommended repairs were actually completed (ask for receipts or a service history)
  • accident, finance or ownership history (those are separate checks)
  • the vehicle’s condition between tests — faults can appear at any time

Advisories, failures and mileage

Since 20 May 2018, testers in Great Britain record defects as Dangerous, Major or Minor, and can still add advisories. That split is set out by DVSA in MOT rule changes: 20 May 2018.

Item on the record What DVSA says it means MOT result
Dangerous A direct and immediate risk to road safety or a serious impact on the environment. Do not drive until it is repaired. Fail
Major It may affect the vehicle’s safety, put other road users at risk or have an impact on the environment. Repair it immediately. Fail
Minor No significant effect on safety or the environment. Repair as soon as possible. Pass
Advisory It could become more serious in the future. Monitor and repair if necessary. Pass

Source: GOV.UK — MOT rule changes: 20 May 2018. For the full defect dictionary, see MOT advisories explained.

A single advisory can be normal wear. Repeated advisories for the same area — tyres, brakes, suspension, lights or emissions — often mean the issue was never properly fixed. Watch for an advisory that later becomes a fail.

Mileage is the tester’s reading at that test. Compare year to year. Consistent progression is what you want. A later test showing a lower figure than an earlier one is a serious warning sign — see how to spot mileage clocking.

If the vehicle failed and you need to know whether a retest is free, see MOT retest costs and DVSA retest rules and the official MOT testing guide (accounts and fees).

GOV.UK also says you cannot drive a vehicle that failed with a dangerous problem until it is repaired. You can be fined up to £2,500, banned from driving and given 3 penalty points for driving with a dangerous fail (Getting an MOT: MOT test result).

MOT history vs MOT status

MOT status answers a narrower question: does the vehicle currently have an MOT certificate, and when does it run out? Use the official GOV.UK MOT status service. You still only need the registration number.

MOT history is the timeline: past pass or fail results, recorded mileage and, where available, defects and advisories. Status tells you if it is currently legal to drive (subject to the other rules). History helps you judge how the vehicle has been maintained.

GOV.UK also says you cannot drive or park a vehicle on the road if the MOT has run out, except to go to a pre-arranged MOT test or to or from somewhere to be repaired. You can be fined up to £1,000 for driving without a valid MOT (Getting an MOT).

How to spot mileage clocking

MOT mileage is not a guaranteed true odometer. Testers can mistype a reading, and a dishonest seller can still alter a dashboard. Use the sequence as a check, then cross-check paperwork. DVSA notes that third parties may use MOT records to check the validity of vehicle mileages (MOT testing guide).

  • Year-on-year decreases: a later MOT showing lower mileage than the year before is a major warning sign.
  • Implausible jumps: very small mileage over many years followed by a big jump, or the reverse.
  • Gaps with low later readings: long periods with no MOTs then an implausibly low figure.
  • Cross-check: compare the DVSA record with service invoices and previous adverts. If you cannot reconcile the numbers, walk away.

A single drop can also be a keying error. DVSA’s Matters of Testing blog explains that testers sometimes type the wrong figure or read a trip meter. Treat an unexplained drop as a stop sign until the owner can show dated evidence. If the figure is simply wrong, see what to do if the record is wrong.

What to do if the MOT record is wrong

GOV.UK has a dedicated process: Report a mistake on your MOT record. Vehicle owners are advised to check their MOT details after a test (MOT testing guide).

You can use the form to report a mistake with:

  • mileage, if the MOT was more than 28 days ago
  • the colour, make, model or fuel type of the vehicle
  • the country where the vehicle was registered
  • the name or location of the MOT centre

The same GOV.UK page also explains how to report:

  • mileage, if the MOT was in the last 28 days (the MOT centre can often correct it and issue a replacement certificate)
  • the MOT due date
  • the MOT history (a missing test, or a test that should not be there)

You cannot change or remove MOT results, defects or advisory problems through that form. If you think the test result itself is wrong, use GOV.UK’s process to appeal the MOT result.

To correct mileage you must upload dated evidence from around the test, such as an invoice, emissions printout, service receipt, service history, or a job card from the MOT centre. GOV.UK says you cannot use an MOT certificate or a photo of the dashboard. The document must show the registration number or VIN, the mileage, and a date within one day of the MOT test. GOV.UK says you will usually get a reply within 8 weeks.

You cannot use the form if the MOT was in Northern Ireland — contact the Driver and Vehicle Agency (DVA) instead.

Red flags to watch for when buying used

Be cautious if you see:

  • Multiple failures for the same component area (for example brakes or emissions)
  • Large mileage jumps or long gaps with no records
  • Repeated advisories with no improvement year on year
  • A sudden “clean” result after years of repeats, with no evidence of work done

Maximum MOT fees are set by DVSA. If you are budgeting a test after purchase, see UK MOT costs for cars and MOT changes in 2026.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check MOT history on GOV.UK?

Go to gov.uk/check-mot-history and enter the vehicle’s registration number (number plate). The official DVSA service is free. You will see past pass or fail results, the mileage recorded at each test, and when the next MOT is due.

What does an MOT history record show?

GOV.UK says the record includes whether the vehicle passed or failed, the mileage recorded when it was tested, and when the next MOT is due. For tests in England, Scotland or Wales you can also download MOT certificates, see failed and minor items, and (with the 11-digit number from the V5C) see where each test was done. A recall may also be shown, depending on the manufacturer.

Is MOT history the same as MOT status?

No. MOT status tells you whether a vehicle currently has an MOT certificate and when it runs out. MOT history shows past test results, recorded mileage and, where available, defects and advisories over time.

Can I check MOT expiry date online?

Yes. GOV.UK MOT history includes when the next MOT is due. You can also use the MOT status service if you only need to know whether the vehicle currently has a certificate and when it runs out.

Is it illegal to drive without an MOT in the UK?

In most cases, yes. GOV.UK says you cannot drive or park the vehicle on the road if the MOT has run out, and you can be fined up to £1,000. The exceptions are driving to a pre-arranged MOT test, or to or from somewhere to be repaired (Getting an MOT).

How do I spot possible mileage clocking on the MOT record?

Compare the mileage recorded at each MOT. A later test showing a lower mileage than an earlier one is a serious warning sign. Also look for implausible jumps or long gaps followed by a surprisingly low reading. Cross-check the DVSA record with service invoices and previous adverts. If you cannot reconcile the numbers, do not buy.

What should I do if the MOT record is wrong?

Use GOV.UK’s Report a mistake on your MOT record form. Within 28 days of the test, the MOT centre can often correct mileage and issue a replacement certificate. After 28 days, DVSA handles mileage and some vehicle-detail errors. You cannot change or remove MOT results, defects or advisories through that form — appeal the result if you think the test itself was wrong. Northern Ireland tests go to the Driver and Vehicle Agency (DVA), not this form.

Official MOT records are published by the UK government: DVSA MOT history service.
